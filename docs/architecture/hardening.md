# Hardening: isolation, limits, keys and authorization (Phase 0)

Part of the architecture notes for Claude Code; the index is in [CLAUDE.md](../../CLAUDE.md).

- **Tenant isolation** (SEC-9). Businesses (tenants) of one edition share a database. Members, the catalogue and cases (0013, 0022) and, since migration 0023, the moderation queue, answers for review, feedback, the Service Designer's threads, runs and lessons, and services' events carry `tenant_id`. Each store takes `{ tenant }` (`createD1ModerationStore`, `createD1ReviewStore`, `createD1FeedbackStore`, `createD1DesignerStore`, `createD1EventStore`, `createD1CaseStore`), and the Worker passes `currentTenant()` (`kiosk(env)`, `designer(env)`, `reviews(env)` and `serviceEvents(env)` are per tenant). A review belongs to its answer's business. `test/tenant-isolation.test.ts` fails when any table has no `tenant_id` unless it is in `SHARED` with a reason, and runs two businesses against each store. Add a table, and the test tells you to decide.
- **Plans** (2026-11-20). The paid plan is its own deployment on a paid Cloudflare Workers plan, with `PLAN=paid` (no limits below). The free deployment (`PLAN=free`, the default) treats everyone alike: a signed-in member of a business is the free plan too. The free plan answers short questions (`FREE_TIER_MAX_QUESTION_CHARS`, 400) with short answers (`FREE_TIER_MAX_TOKENS`, 400: the model is capped and told to keep to about 0.4 words a token, `briefRules` in core `agent/prompts.ts`, `ask(..., { brief })`), and sends longer questions (413), longer answers, and drafting a whole service with AI (402) to the paid plan; `UPGRADE_URL` is the page the messages point to. Nothing exempts anyone from the allowance below.
- **Limits** (SEC-10, 11). `quota/` (core, export `./quota`): a sliding log (`createQuota(store).take(key, { limit, windowMs })`, `allows`, `note`), `ipKey` (hashed address; IPv6 by /64; none = a local run, not limited), memory and D1 stores (platform `quota_hits`, migration 0010). The Worker's `worker/limits.ts` `modelGate(c, env, { chars, long })`: the paid deployment and `FREE_TIER_QUESTIONS=0` pass; anyone else is the free plan: `FREE_TIER_QUESTIONS` per `FREE_TIER_WINDOW_MINUTES` per IP (3 per 40), within `FREE_TIER_DAILY` for the deployment, forced onto `freeTierSelection(env)` (the first configured catalogue model marked `free`, or `FREE_TIER_MODEL`) and the lean harness (chat), and answered 429 with `Retry-After` or 503 in words that offer Kiosk only. It guards chat and extract (short questions) and refuses designer drafts and consent drafts as paid-plan work; flag and search only get the burst limit. `withIpBurst` adds a per-IP minute limit (the rate-limit binding, keyed by hashed IP) to those routes, because a per-session limit stops nobody who throws the cookie away. `caseCall` counts wrong case references per IP (`CASE_REF_MISSES` in 15 minutes). `capabilities.freeTier` (questions, minutes, words, chars, upgrade) tells Preferences what the free plan allows.
- **Kiosk only by default.** `composables/settings.ts` `WEB_AGENT_DEFAULTS` starts a new visitor with `knowledge.answers: "local"`. Saved settings keep their choice.
- **Sealing keys** (SEC-12). `identity/crypto.ts` `seal(secret, text, purpose)` writes `v2.<purpose>.<iv>.<ciphertext>` with an HKDF key per purpose (and the purpose as authenticated data); `unseal` opens that and the original format. Purposes: `signing-key`, `pending-claim`, `oidc-client`, `connector`. `worker/secrets.ts` seals connectors' secrets with `SEALING_SECRET` (else `SESSION_SECRET`) and tries both when opening.
- **Authorization** (SEC-13). `worker/test/authorization.test.ts` asks every route as an anonymous caller and compares the kind of answer with `authorization.snapshot.json` (`UPDATE_ROUTE_SNAPSHOT=1` rewrites it after you have checked the change); `OPEN_BY_DESIGN` lists what may be open and why. The worker tests load `index.ts` in Node with `cloudflare:workers` stubbed (`worker/test/shims`).
- **Compiled services.** `library/index.ts` `compiledOnce` keeps each published revision's compile per isolate (400 services), so `published()` costs the database reads, not a recompile of every service.
- **TanStack Query.** `lib/queries.ts`: `queryClient` (15 s stale time, no refetch on focus, one retry only for server errors), `useApiQuery(key, path)`, `useApiMutation(call, refreshKeys)`, `errorText(...)`. Plugin registered in `main.ts`. Used by `BusinessLinks.vue`, `JoinLinks.vue` and `ApprovedSites.vue`; move other panels one at a time, never state a person is typing.

## Data lifetime and trust (decided 2026-11-20; step 1 built)

The platform is a multi-business, cloud-moderated standard process; people's devices hold their data (local-first, relay-style like WhatsApp). Health belongs to clinux, so nothing here is treated differently for health. Specs: `13-foundations.md` (STO, RET), `09-privacy-storage-security.md` (PRV-7..10).

- **Everything is transient.** A record on the server is deleted 30 days after its `created_at` (not after last activity), on every table that holds records: cases and their events, threads, answers for review, feedback, the moderation queue, service events, designer runs. One rule for all types, no per-class periods. A daily purge deletes by `created_at` in small chunks (needs an index on `created_at`); it does not loop over businesses. A delivered item may go sooner. The audit chain is pruned from its oldest end with a checkpoint hash of the removed prefix so the rest still verifies.
- **Services and their configuration are kept, on both plans** (corrected 2026-11-20; it replaces "a service and its configuration expire in the same 30-day window"). They are designed and changed on the server (the Service Designer and the catalogue), and no device holds a copy, so expiring them would delete the design for good. Only runtime data (cases, their events, service events, and the other transient tables) is transient. The catalogue tables are `identity` in `TABLE_LIFETIME`. A long-idle business's service YAML can be archived to object storage (R2) and restored on return; not built (see the handoff's open items).
- **A sync is a runtime-data import, limited on the free plan to 5 a year per account**, counted on the server (`SYNC_PER_YEAR`; step 3). A device (or a file) puts back the records it holds, and an import writes fresh `created_at` values for cases, so keeping data alive by syncing every 30 days (12 a year) is not possible. The count is shown to the person ("3 of 5 left this year"). The paid plan has no such limit.
- **Only the paid plan persists data, and retention is set per service**, not per field or field type. The service author declares one `retain` period for the service (set in the service design), and every record the service captures is kept for that period; without it, the 30-day rule applies. The author, not the platform, decides the period; the platform is not responsible for the author's audit or statutory duties and does not confirm periods. On the free plan a service that declares retention publishes with a notice that its records expire at 30 days.
- **No field classes** (corrected 2026-11-20): the `local`/`shared`/`retained` field idea is dropped. Retention is per service, as above. Separating a record into an envelope (what the server routes and counts) and a payload is only groundwork for end-to-end encryption, so it waits with it.
- **End-to-end encryption waits.** Roles are addressed ("any storekeeper"), so it needs group keys, rotation, recovery and moderator access. If it is taken up, the envelope/payload split comes first (named individual recipients first, role groups last). The free relay promises deletion at 30 days, not E2E; chat to a hosted model is never E2E.
- **Trust rule for checks.** The sender's device may run the journeys engine (validation, conditions, summaries) and attach the result as advisory. The server verifies only signature, membership, schema and quota, and never recomputes content; the recipient's device re-runs what matters to it. Timers, case expiry and scheduled routines need something always on: the paid server or a business's yaxb-box. Server-side rules stay declarative (journeys expressions). The Deep Agents `execute` tool stays unregistered; if code is ever wanted, run QuickJS (WASM, no network, time and memory limits) on the device only, on request. The installed `deepagents` 1.16.3 only mentions a QuickJS REPL for skills; the interpreter package is not installed and not checked.
- **Backups.** D1's point-in-time backups keep deleted rows about 30 days longer, so "deleted at day 30" means gone from backups by about day 60. The terms say so.
- **Paid deployment.** Moving a business from free to paid: the services and their configuration are re-created on the paid site from their YAML (the editor), connector secrets are re-sealed (or `SEALING_SECRET` and signing keys are shared), people sign in again if the platform databases differ, and the records come across by the runtime-data export and import (step 4). Business links cannot cross deployments. A lapsed paid plan gets a 30-day grace period, read-only with an export offered; after it, the free-plan rules apply (30 days from creation).
- **Built (step 1, 2026-11-20).** `stores/lifetime.ts` `TABLE_LIFETIME` gives every table a class: `transient` (30 days from `created_at`), `service` (cases, their events, service events: 30 days until a service can declare `retain`) or `identity` (kept, with the reason). `test/lifetime.test.ts` fails for a table with no class (edition and platform databases), checks the purge column is indexed (migration 0024 adds the missing indexes), and runs the purge. `purgeExpired(db, now, {chunk, maxChunks, serviceDays})` deletes by age in chunks of 500 (up to 40 a table a run, the rest the next day), children before parents (a case's events, an answer's reviews), and prunes the audit chain from its oldest end (`pruneAudit`; the oldest remaining record's `prev_hash` is the checkpoint of the removed prefix, so `verifyAuditChain` still checks the rest; `audit_heads` is untouched). The Worker runs it from a second cron (`17 3 * * *`, `PURGE_CRON`) through `worker/purge.ts` `purgeAll`, on every bound edition database, no loop over businesses. On `PLAN=paid` (`retain: true`) a service's records live for its `retain_days` (see step 2 below). The agent's memory (`kv`) and conversations (`threads`) exist only in Server storage mode, which is the paid plan's (`/api/agent/chat` answers 402 for `storage: "server"` on `PLAN=free`); they are kept on `PLAN=paid` (`keepOnPaid`) and purged on free (a leftover), otherwise they live on the device. `threads` is counted from its last write (it has no `created_at`); `design_lessons` and the service catalogue are `identity` (their expiry with the service configuration is a later step).
- **Built (step 2, 2026-11-20): the service's `retain` period.** `library_entries.retain_days` (migration 0025; null = 30), set per service, not in the YAML, like `storage`: `setEntryRetain` (its author or an editor; whole days 30 to 7300; audited as `library.entry_retain`), `PUT /api/library/entries/:id/retain`, published as `retain_days`. The author sets it in the service's Consent view, shown when the service keeps records on the server; the free plan shows a notice that records expire at 30 days whatever it says, and accepts the setting so a service moves to paid unchanged. The purge (`retain: true` on `PLAN=paid`) deletes a case, its events and a service event only when older than 30 days and older than its service's `retain_days` (looked up by `cases.entry_id`, `service_events.service`; a service with none, or deleted, gets 30). The Legal Guardian's consent wording for server storage says "kept for N days after it is made, then deleted" (`ConsentFacts.retainDays`; the free plan always says 30). The platform doesn't check the period against any law (decided); the panel says so.
- **Built (step 3, 2026-11-20): the sync allowance.** `quota/sync-allowance.ts` (`createSyncAllowance`, platform database migration 0011 `sync_uses`): per account, a rolling 365 days, one statement to take a use so two at once can't both take the last one. `SYNC_PER_YEAR` (default 5, 0 = no limit) applies on `PLAN=free` only; the paid plan has none (`worker/sync-allowance.ts` `syncLimit`). `GET /api/sync/allowance` gives `{limit, used, left, freeAgainAt}` for the signed-in account; Preferences → Storage shows "3 of 5 left this year" (`SyncAllowance.vue`, hidden with no limit or signed out). **Nothing consumes it yet:** the device-to-server push doesn't exist, and step 4's import is it. Call `takeSync(env, account.sub)` before the import writes anything, and answer 429 with `freeAgainAt` when `ok` is false.
- **Built (step 4, 2026-11-20): runtime-data export and import.** `records/index.ts` (`./records`): `exportRecords(db, tenant, {service})` writes a versioned bundle (`format: "yaxb-records"`, `version: 1`) of one business's cases, their events and service events, with the services' ids and document ids; `parseRecords` checks every row (strict schema, JSON columns must parse, at most 5000 rows); `importRecords(db, tenant, bundle, {plan})` adds the rows to the importing business and never overwrites or takes over (`INSERT OR IGNORE`; an id that exists, whoever owns it, is left alone, so a second import adds nothing, and an event is only attached to a case of the same business). Each row is matched to the business's own service by the service's document id, so it works on another deployment; a row of a service the business doesn't have is reported (add the service, import again). A case's dependency links are never exported. Clock: on the free plan a case gets a fresh `created_at` and any row older than 30 days is skipped; on the paid plan rows keep their times and are skipped only when older than the service's `retain_days`. Rows go in through `json_each` batches (200 rows or 900 KB a statement), so a file takes a few statements, not one per row (a Worker request has few database calls). `GET /api/records/export[?service=]` and `POST /api/records/import` (`routes/records.ts`) are for administrators; on `PLAN=free` the import calls `takeSync` after the file checks out and before anything is written (429 with the free-again date when the year's allowance is used; without an account the business is counted). Screen: Administration → Records (`RecordsTransfer.vue`). A copy of the database is never the export: businesses of an edition share one D1 database, so a whole-database restore (Time Travel on the cloud, Litestream on the box) is the operator's disaster recovery and touches every business.
- **Built (step 5, 2026-11-20): size alerts and the sweep queue.** `stores/size.ts`: D1 reports a database's size in each query's meta (`databaseBytes`), `sizeLevel` is `warn` at 70% and `critical` at 90% of the limit (500 MB free, 10 GB paid; `D1_LIMIT_MB` overrides), `biggestTenants` counts rows per business in the big tables (counts only, never content). The daily cron (after the purge) runs `worker/db-size.ts` `checkSizes` on every bound edition database: a warning or critical reading is logged (`msg: "database size"`, with the biggest businesses) and each reading is kept in the platform database (`platform_meta` `db-size:<edition>`). `GET /api/admin/database` (site administrator) and Administration → Database size show the reading, with no business names. Nothing deletes anything: the answer to a full database is records expiring on their own, then moving the biggest business to its own database. **Sweep queue:** `routines.ts` `sweep` builds one job per business (`routines`, or `cases` for dependencies past their time). With a Cloudflare Queue bound as `SWEEP_QUEUE` (the paid plan; commented in `wrangler.jsonc`, create it with `wrangler queues create yaxb-sweep`) the cron only enqueues and the Worker's `queue()` runs the jobs in parallel, each retried after 60 s (3 times); without it the jobs run in the cron one after another, as before. Not enabled in the repo's `wrangler.jsonc`, because a deploy fails if the queue doesn't exist.
- **Build order:** (1) every table declares a class (transient, kept per service, identity), enforced by a test like the tenant-isolation one, plus the daily purge (done); (2) a service-level `retain` period in the designer, honoured by the purge on the paid plan (done, below); (3) the sync counter (done; the push that calls it comes with step 4); (4) runtime-data export and import (done, below); (5) database-size alerts and the queued cron for paid (done, above).
